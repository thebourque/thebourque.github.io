What got "eaten" by a child?

GUMBO draws project areas at different sizes — AG (biggest, an approved geography) down through DA, CDA, SB, to CA (smallest, the actual construction job). A parent is any bigger shape; a child is a smaller shape sitting inside it.

The bite

When engineering breaks a parent into smaller, more specific children (e.g. a DA broken into CAs), those children now cover ground that used to belong to the parent. The parent's leftover on this map is never the whole original outline — it's parent_polygon − union(all overlapping child polygons). Whatever the children now cover is subtracted out so it isn't drawn or counted twice.

If a DA has been fully carved into CAs, its leftover is an empty shape — that's a good sign (engineering finished breaking it down), not a bug. A real leftover sliver means unengineered ground nobody has claimed yet.

Who eats whom

Finest wins, working up the ladder: CA eats nothing (nothing is finer than it) → SB eats CA → CDA eats SB+CA → DA eats CDA+SB+CA → AG eats everything under it. A CA never loses ground to anyone; the biggest umbrellas (AG, DA) get chewed on the most.

A second bite, only on this map

After the CA/CDA/DA/AG leftover is computed, live FSA and marketing polygons take another bite (that ground has already gone commercial — no longer "leftover to build," it's "already served"). Leftover shapes that lose everything to this second bite are dropped from the map entirely; tiny slivers below a size floor are dropped too as rounding noise, not real leftover.
